## 1. Purpose and scope

This plan sets out how The Haven keeps teaching, learner welfare and safeguarding running, or restores them quickly, when something goes wrong. It is written for the people who will use it under pressure: it says who decides, what must be restored first and how fast, what we do in the first hour and the first day of each kind of disruption, and who tells whom.

The Haven has no premises. All lessons are live online, all staff work remotely, and every system we depend on is cloud-hosted. That removes the risks that dominate most schools' plans (fire, flood, loss of a building) and replaces them with others: platform outages, loss of connectivity, cyber incidents, supplier changes, and the dependence of a small team on a handful of people. The plan is built around those risks.

The plan covers all Haven provision: full-time and part-time timetabled lessons, the Back to Balance programme, mentoring and welfare check-ins, Passion Projects and clubs, examinations, and the records and communications that sit behind them. It applies to employed staff, freelance educators and volunteers. Autistic Girls Network holds a separate Business Continuity Plan for the charity as a whole; where a disruption affects both (for example loss of the Proprietor, or a cyber incident affecting shared Google Workspace), the two plans run together and the charity's Incident Lead and The Haven's Incident Lead agree who leads.

### 1.1 What this plan means for learners

Most of our learners were out of education for months or years before joining The Haven, and many experienced school as a place that stopped without warning. For them a disruption is more than a lost lesson. Every decision made under this plan is tested against four commitments to learners, in this order.

**Safeguarding does not pause.** During the school day the DSL or Deputy DSL responds to any concern within 4 hours, whatever else is disrupted. Concerns are recorded on a secure offline template if systems are down, and the safeguarding contacts for each learner's home local authority are held off-platform. See sections 3 and 5.6.

**Welfare comes before curriculum.** Every learner or family hears from an adult they already know within 24 hours of any disruption they would notice, in calm, plain language that says what has happened and what happens next. No learner is disadvantaged by a disruption on our side. If teaching capacity is reduced, a welfare-first timetable (daily check-in, one core lesson a day, mentoring) replaces the full timetable until it can be restored. See sections 5.1, 5.7 and 5.8 and Appendix C.

**Education continues.** Lessons run on the fallback platform within 24 hours and the full timetable is restored within 72 hours. A learner's own educator, or a colleague the learner has already met, takes the lesson wherever possible, and learning tasks stay available in Google Classroom. A learner who cannot connect is contacted the same day and helped to reconnect or given a task to work on in their own time. Commissioned hours lost to a disruption on our side are made up or agreed with the commissioner. See sections 5.1, 5.2 and 5.7.

**Support arrangements stay in place.** Support set out in a learner's EHCP or placement plan continues through any disruption. The SENDCo reviews each affected learner's provision within two working days, agrees any temporary adjustment with the family and the commissioner, and makes sure reasonable adjustments and access arrangements carry over to the fallback platform or a cover educator. Mentoring sessions are kept or rescheduled within the week, and annual reviews and commissioner meetings go ahead by video or phone on their planned dates. Changes are explained in advance wherever possible, by a familiar adult, with time for the learner to process them.

### 2.2 When the plan is activated

The Incident Lead activates the plan when a disruption is likely to stop a critical activity (section 3) for longer than its tolerable outage; when there is a risk to the safety of a learner or a member of staff; when learner or family data may have been lost or exposed; or when our ability to meet a contractual, statutory or examination obligation is at risk. Anything that can be fixed within a single lesson or a single working day is handled as business as usual and recorded in the incident log (Appendix B) so that patterns are visible at review.

### 2.3 Decision authority

The Head may cancel or move individual lessons, switch platforms, arrange cover and commit expenditure up to the limit in Autistic Girls Network's Financial Procedures. Suspending all teaching for more than two days, any decision affecting examinations, any public statement and any expenditure above the limit require the Proprietor. The Haven Education Committee is informed of every activation within two working days and receives a written report at its next meeting; the Trustee Board receives the same report through the Proprietor.

## 5. Response by scenario

### 5.1 Classroom or video platform outage

- **First 15 minutes:** the educator confirms the problem is not local (second device or phone hotspot), posts the agreed fallback message to learners through the backup channel (Google Classroom stream or email to families) and moves the lesson to the fallback video platform. Learners and families already hold the fallback instructions in their welcome pack.
- **First hour:** the Head or Operations Lead confirms whether the outage affects one lesson or the whole timetable, tells all educators which platform to use for the rest of the day, and posts a notice to all families. The Systems Architect contacts the supplier and logs the incident.
- **First day:** if the primary platform is still down at the end of the day, tomorrow's timetable is published on the fallback platform through Haven Today, and commissioners with learners affected are told by email.
- **Recovery:** teaching continues on the fallback platform until the primary is restored and checked. Target: lessons running within 24 hours, full timetable within 72 hours. Attendance is recorded on the offline template and backfilled.
- **Standing mitigation:** every educator has accounts and a tested login on both platforms; every lesson plan and resource is saved in Google Drive, not only inside a platform; a termly five-minute fallback drill is run in each group so that switching platforms is familiar to learners rather than alarming.

### 5.2 Educator absent at short notice

- **Before 8.30am on the day:** the educator tells the Head and Operations Lead by the agreed route in Slack. If the absence is discovered only when the lesson fails to start, learners wait no more than ten minutes before the Operations Lead or Head joins and explains.
- **Cover options, in order:** a colleague, wherever possible one the learners already know, takes the lesson using the shared plan; two small groups are merged under one educator; the Head or Operations Lead runs a welfare and independent-work session using the Passion Projects or Google Classroom tasks; the lesson is moved to later in the week. A lesson is cancelled only when none of these is possible, and families are told before the start time wherever that can be done.
- **Longer absence (more than a week):** the Head reallocates the timetable, brings in a freelance educator only if necessary (multiple consecutive absences) and tells affected commissioners within two working days, because EOTAS and Alternative Provision contracts specify hours.
- **Standing mitigation:** lesson plans and resources follow a common format in Google Drive, so that any Haven educator can pick up a lesson at short notice. Cover is taken, wherever possible, by an educator the learners already know. Where learners will be meeting a cover educator for the first time, they and their families are told who it will be before the lesson wherever that can be done.

### 5.3 Loss of the Head and DSL, the Proprietor or the Systems Architect

- **Day 1 (Head and DSL):** the Deputy DSL becomes DSL and the Operations Lead becomes acting Incident Lead; the Proprietor tells the Education Committee Co-Chairs and the Trustee Board; the Proprietor confirms who now holds authority for safeguarding escalation, timetable decisions and external communication. The published DSL contact on the website and in family information is updated the same day.
- **Week 1:** educators, families and commissioners are told who their contact is now. No public announcement is made unless the absence will be prolonged. Upcoming commitments (annual reviews, commissioner returns, exam deadlines, Ofsted OEAS correspondence) are listed from the Head's handover note and allocated.
- **Week 4:** if the absence is expected to exceed half a term, the Proprietor and Education Committee agree interim leadership (an acting Head from the educator team, a secondment or an interim appointment) and a budget for it.
- **Proprietor:** the Autistic Girls Network plan (section 5.1) applies; the Head continues to run the school and the Chair of Trustees becomes the Head's line of accountability.
- **Systems Architect:** the haven-architecture repository documents every system; the Proprietor holds admin rights on every platform; an external IT contractor can be engaged within five working days.
- **Standing mitigation:** the Head keeps a current handover note (learners of concern, commissioner contacts, deadlines, where things are) reviewed each half term; no supplier, platform or regulator relationship depends on a single login.

### 5.4 Cyber attack, account compromise or data breach

- **Contain (first hour):** the Systems Architect or Proprietor changes passwords, revokes sessions and removes the affected account or device from Google Workspace and every platform. Live lessons continue on an unaffected platform where safe. A device that may hold evidence is not wiped.
- **Assess (first 24 hours):** establish what data was involved, whose and how sensitive. Learner data is always treated as high risk: our learners are children, many with EHCPs, and some have safeguarding histories. Record everything in the incident log.
- **Notify:** the Proprietor decides, following the Data Breach Procedure, whether the breach is reportable to the ICO within 72 hours and whether learners and families must be told without undue delay. Commissioners whose learners' data is involved are told the same day. The cyber insurer (£1 million cyber cover) is told at once, as the policy requires. Where a supplier reports the breach, Autistic Girls Network remains the controller and the same steps apply.
- **Recover:** restore from Google Workspace and platform backups or exports; rebuild access with new credentials and enforced multi-factor authentication; run a lessons-learned review within two weeks and report to the Education Committee.

### 5.5 Supplier change or withdrawal

- **Trigger:** a platform announces closure, a material price rise, a change of terms we cannot accept (for example AI features processing learner data without an acceptable agreement) or fails to agree a compliant data processing agreement.
- **Response:** within one week the Systems Architect exports all learner data and content from the supplier and confirms the export opens; the Head and Proprietor choose the replacement from the pre-assessed options; a migration plan with a date is agreed and families are told what will change and when. Nothing is switched off until the replacement has been used for at least one full week of lessons.
- **Standing mitigation:** a data processing agreement is in place with every supplier before learner data is entered; the systems register (section 7) names an alternative for each platform; the 2026 migrations are written up as a procedure in the haven-architecture repository.

### 5.6 Safeguarding incident during or around a live lesson

- **In the lesson:** the educator follows the Online Learner Supervision Policy and the Online Safety Policy: stay calm, keep the learner on the call if they are at risk, mute or remove other participants if necessary, do not promise confidentiality, and message the DSL on the agreed channel while the lesson continues. If there is immediate danger, the educator calls 999 and gives the learner's address from the register.
- **Within the hour:** the DSL takes over contact with the learner and family, makes any referral to the home local authority front door, and records the concern on the safeguarding system. The Proprietor is told the same day; the LADO is contacted where an adult working for The Haven is involved.
- **Continuity actions:** cover is arranged for the educator for the rest of the day if they need it; any recording of the session is preserved and access restricted; where a commissioned learner is involved, the commissioner is told the same day; an individual is suspended from contact with learners while any allegation is looked into. Outside school hours and in holidays, The Haven does not run an on-call DSL service, in line with other schools; the Head (or the Proprietor as backup) monitors the safeguarding mailbox and phone and responds to Council, commissioner or police contact within 24 hours, and families are told that The Haven is not an emergency service and are signposted to 999, NHS 111 and Childline.

### 5.7 Learner cannot connect

- **Same day:** when a learner does not join a lesson they were expected at, the educator records it in the register and carries on teaching the group. The educator or the Operations Lead contacts the family by message or email once the lesson has ended, and in any case the same day, to check the learner is safe and well, help with reconnecting and share the lesson task so the learner can work on it in their own time. The reason is recorded.
- **Same week:** where the barrier is equipment or connectivity, the Operations Lead assists with connection, or discusses options with the commissioner. Persistent non-attendance follows the Attendance and Engagement Policy, which treats Barriers to Attendance as something to understand, not a behaviour to correct.

### 5.8 Widespread illness or national emergency

- **Response:** The Haven is remote by design and continues to teach. If several educators are ill at once, the Head replaces the academic timetable with a reduced welfare-first timetable (daily community check-in, one core lesson a day, mentoring) and tells families and commissioners what to expect. Staff wellbeing sessions are offered and lower-priority work is paused.

### 5.9 Examination disruption

- **Response:** The Haven is not an exam centre. Learners are entered as private candidates at external centres, so the risks we manage are a centre closing, withdrawing or declining our learners, a learner being unable to attend or being disrupted on the day, and a missed entry deadline. The Examinations Policy takes precedence. If a centre withdraws, the Exams Officer contacts the alternative centre identified for that learner the same day and confirms the transfer of entries and access arrangements with the awarding body. If a learner cannot sit or is disrupted on the day, the Exams Officer asks the centre to apply for special consideration the same day and tells the learner and family what will happen next within 24 hours. Access arrangements evidence and centre confirmations are held in Google Drive so they survive any platform failure.
- **Standing mitigation:** entries are made as early as the centre allows, an alternative centre is identified for every learner at the point of entry, and the Exams Officer confirms arrangements with each centre at least four weeks before the first paper.

## 9. Training, testing and review

- Every educator, volunteer and member of staff is shown this plan at induction, knows the fallback platform procedure and the safeguarding escalation route, and knows where the live version is (the policy site and the Haven Admin folder).
- A platform-switch drill is run in every group once a term so that moving to the fallback platform is routine for learners.
- A tabletop exercise is run every twelve months with the Leadership Team and at least one Governor, rotating the scenario: platform outage in the middle of a teaching day; loss of the Head; cyber incident involving learner data; safeguarding incident in a live lesson; examination disruption. The exercise is minuted and any gaps become actions with owners and dates.
- The Leadership Team reviews the plan every six months; the Head reviews it in full annually and after any activation and reports to the Education Committee. The systems register (section 7) and contacts (section 8) are checked each term.
- Review log: v01.26, October 2025 (original plan, two scenarios). v11.26, November 2026: full rewrite to an operational format with recovery objectives, a risk register, scenario responses, a systems register and testing schedule, following feedback from a local authority framework evaluation. First tabletop exercise scheduled for December 2026.

## Appendix A: Immediate response checklist

1. Is anyone at risk? Deal with safety first: 999 if there is immediate danger, then the DSL if a learner is involved.
2. Tell the Incident Lead (Head) or, if unavailable, the Operations Lead. They decide whether to activate the plan.
3. Contain the problem: switch platforms, secure accounts, pause the affected activity, preserve evidence.
4. Start the incident log (Appendix B): what, when, who knows, what has been done.
5. Work out which critical activities (section 3) are affected and their recovery objectives.
6. Tell the people who need to know (section 6), starting with learners and families whose lessons are affected today.
7. Check the clocks: ICO 72 hours; insurer notification; exam centre and awarding body deadlines; commissioner same-day notice.
8. Restore, then review: record what happened and what changes, and report to the Education Committee.
